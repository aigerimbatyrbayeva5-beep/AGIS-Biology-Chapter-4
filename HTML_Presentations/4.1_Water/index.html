Key term
Surface tension — a property of the surface of a liquid that allows it to resist an external force.
Molecules at the surface hold together (cohesion). The surface acts like a thin skin, so a light animal does not sink.
Quick check
What would happen if the strider were much heavier?
The surface could not resist such a big force. The strider would sink.

Explain (12:30–14:00). Return to the big question. Press → to show the forces, → again for the explanation and quick check.

Key term wording is from the textbook (p. 56). “Thin skin” is a comparison, not a real skin — say “like a skin”.

Quick check — expected: it would sink; the surface can only resist a small force.

3D view. The front of the water is cut away, so students see the dips from the side. Revealing the answer (button or A) plays a 3-second demo: the strider gets heavier, the dips get deeper, the surface gives way and the strider sinks. Hide the answer (A) or press R to bring the strider back.

Drops on a coin
Капли на монете
Materials
glass of water · dropper · coin · paper towel
Pre-lab questions
1. What is cohesion?

Predict first
How many drops will stay on the coin before water spills?
I predict ___ drops because …
⚠ Safety: keep water away from sockets and devices · wipe spills at once · wash hands after the lab.
Procedure
  1. Place the coin on a flat surface. Dry it with the towel.
  2. Slowly drip water onto the coin — one drop at a time.
  3. Count the drops. Write the number when the coin is full.
  4. Keep dripping. Write the number when water spills.

Main activity (18:00–30:00). Pairs. 1 min: pre-lab questions orally. 1 min: teacher models holding the dropper vertically and squeezing gently — one drop at a time. 1 min: students write a prediction on the Lab 2 worksheet. 6 min: experiment (repeat if time — record each trial). 3 min: results and post-lab questions (next two scenes).

Roles: Student A drips, Student B counts aloud and records; swap for trial 2.

Record actual observations only — no invented numbers. Results vary with coin size, dropper and how gently drops are added; this is useful for later discussion of method.

ATL — Research (information literacy): accurate recording of counts. ATL — Thinking: connect the dome to cohesion and surface tension.

Exit ticketWORK ALONE · 3 MINUTES
Exit ticket
Билет на выход: ответь сам
1 · Recall
What is surface tension?
A property of the surface of a liquid that allows it to resist an external force.
2 · Understanding
Explain why ice floating on a lake helps fish survive the winter.
Ice is less dense, so it floats and the lake freezes only on top. The water under the ice stays liquid (about 4 °C), so fish can live there.
3 · Application
A tall tree needs water in its top leaves. Use cohesion and adhesion to explain how the water gets there.
Water molecules stick to each other (cohesion) and to the walls of the stem tubes (adhesion). So water moves up from the soil, through the roots and stem, to the leaves.
