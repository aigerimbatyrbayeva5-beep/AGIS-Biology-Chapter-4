Think
A plant lacks phosphorus. Which one-nutrient fertilizer could help?
Superphosphate (a phosphorus fertilizer).

Explain (17:40–18:00). Textbook p. 63: “Inorganic fertilizers contain one or more nutrients. One nutrient fertilizer contains only one type of element. For example, nitrogen fertilizers (ammonium sulfate, urine, nitrate), potassium fertilizers (potassium chloride), phosphorus fertilizers (superphosphate). Multi-nutrient fertilizers contain trace elements such as nitrogen, potassium, phosphorus, as well as boron and manganese.”

Two textbook wording issues are corrected on the slide and logged: “urine” in a list of synthetic nitrogen fertilizers is read as urea; nitrogen, potassium and phosphorus are macroelements, not trace elements — only boron and manganese are called trace elements here.

ApplyPLANT DOCTOR · RESPONSIBLE USE
Plant doctor
Доктор растений
Case 1
A tomato plant has few, small leaves, and its fruits do not ripen. Which element is missing? Which fertilizer could help?
Phosphorus. A phosphorus fertilizer, e.g. superphosphate (or a multi-nutrient fertilizer).
Case 2
A plant has yellow leaves that shrink. It flowers slowly and has few fruits. What is your diagnosis?
Nitrogen deficiency. A nitrogen fertilizer (e.g. ammonium sulfate, nitrate) or an organic fertilizer such as manure or compost.
Discuss · literacy p. 63
What are the problems with overuse of fertilizers? Is “more fertilizer” always better?
No. Too much can harm plants and soil and wash into water. Use the right amount at the right time.
Debatable: To what extent can people meet plants’ needs while using fertilizers responsibly? I think … because ….

Apply (30:00–37:00). Cases 1–2 (4 min, pairs): unfamiliar plant cases — Criterion A.ii/A.iii practice (apply and justify). Discussion (3 min): textbook Literacy question 2 and the unit Debatable question (global context: globalization and sustainability).

Expected: Case 1 — phosphorus (fruit does not ripen, smaller and fewer leaves) → superphosphate. Case 2 — nitrogen (yellow, shrinking leaves, slow flowering, fewer fruits) → nitrogen or organic fertilizer.
The overuse question is asked in the textbook but not answered there. Accept reasoned ideas (e.g. harm to plants, soil or water; wasted money). Misconception (Unit Planner): “fertilizer always improves growth regardless of dose”. Literacy Q1 (“What time of year is fertilizer applied?”) can be homework — answers will vary by crop and climate.
