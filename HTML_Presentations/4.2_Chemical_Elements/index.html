ApplyELEMENT DETECTIVES · NEW SITUATIONS
Element detectives
Детективы элементов
Case 1
A patient’s blood cannot carry enough oxygen. Which element may be missing? Why?
Iron (Fe). Iron is part of hemoglobin, the blood protein that transports oxygen.
Case 2
A 12-year-old is growing fast. Which two elements help build bones and teeth?
Calcium (Ca) and phosphorus (P) — both are found in bones and teeth.
Case 3 · challenge
Nerve signals in a person are weak. Which elements form nerve impulses?
Sodium (Na), potassium (K) and chlorine (Cl). Potassium has a main role in the nervous system.
Use: “I think ___ is missing because ___ is part of / helps ___.”

Apply (30:00–37:00). Pairs choose a case (Cases 1–2 for everyone, Case 3 as challenge), answer orally with the frame, then reveal. This practises Criterion A.ii (apply knowledge in unfamiliar situations).

Expected: Case 1 — iron (hemoglobin). Case 2 — calcium and phosphorus. Case 3 — Na, K, Cl.

SoI link: “Interactions among substances within living systems support essential functions” — each element supports a function; a lack affects the system.

These are science reasoning cases, not medical advice. If a student mentions a health problem, refer to the school nurse or parents.
Exit ticketWORK ALONE · 3 MINUTES
Exit ticket
Билет на выход: ответь сам
1 · Recall
Name the four main elements that make up 96% of the body.
Oxygen (O), carbon (C), hydrogen (H) and nitrogen (N).
2 · Understanding
What is the difference between a macroelement and a microelement? Give one example of each.
A macroelement is needed in large amounts (e.g. calcium). A microelement is needed in small amounts (e.g. iron).
3 · Application
A person’s muscles do not contract well and their blood does not clot normally. Which element could be missing? Explain.
Calcium (Ca). Calcium acts in muscle contraction and in blood clotting.

Exit ticket (37:00–40:00). Printed slips, answered alone. Collect at the door.

Success criteria: Q1 all four elements. Q2 uses “large/small amounts” + correct examples (macro: C, O, H, N, Ca, Na, Cl, S, K, Mg, P; micro: Fe, Mn, F, Se, Cu, Cr, Co, Zn, Mo, Si, Sn, V, I). Q3 names calcium and links it to both functions.

Sort into secure / developing / needs reteaching. Revisit macro vs micro at the start of Lesson 4.4.
